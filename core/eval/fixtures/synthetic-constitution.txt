CHAPTER 3
CITIZENSHIP

35 Zimbabwean citizenship and nationality
(1) Persons are Zimbabwean citizens by birth, descent or registration.
(2) Citizens have duties as well as rights, including loyalty to the country.

36 Citizenship by birth
(1) Persons are Zimbabwean citizens by birth if they were born in Zimbabwe and, when they were
born, either their mother or their father was a Zimbabwean citizen.
(2) A person born outside Zimbabwe is not a citizen by birth under this section.
(3) A child found in Zimbabwe who is, or appears to be, less than fifteen years of age, and whose
parents are not known, is presumed to be a citizen by birth.

37 Citizenship by descent
(1) A person born outside Zimbabwe is a citizen by descent if one parent was a citizen by birth
or registration at the time of the birth.
(2) The registration of such a birth must be made within the time an Act of Parliament sets.

CHAPTER 4
DECLARATION OF RIGHTS

Part 2
Fundamental human rights and freedoms

48 Right to life
(1) Every person has the right to life.
(2) A law may allow the death penalty only for murder committed in aggravating circumstances,
and the court must keep a discretion whether to impose it.
(3) The penalty must not be imposed or carried out on a woman, or on a person under twenty-one.

51 Right to human dignity
Every person has inherent dignity in their private and public life, and the right to have that
dignity respected and protected.

52 Right to personal security
Every person has the right to bodily and psychological integrity, including freedom from all
forms of violence from public or private sources.

53 Freedom from torture
No person may be subjected to physical or psychological torture or to cruel, inhuman or
degrading treatment or punishment.

55 Freedom from forced labour
No person may be made to perform forced or compulsory labour.

56 Equality and non-discrimination
(1) All persons are equal before the law and have the right to equal protection and benefit of the law.
(2) Women and men have the right to equal treatment, including equal opportunities in political,
economic, cultural and social spheres.
(3) Every person has the right not to be treated in an unfairly discriminatory manner on grounds
such as nationality, race, tribe, place of birth, language, religious belief, political affiliation,
sex, gender, marital status, age, pregnancy or disability.
(4) Treatment is unfairly discriminatory if people are subjected to a condition or disability that
others are not.

57 Right to privacy
Every person has the right to privacy, which includes not having their home, premises or property
entered without their permission, their person searched, their possessions seized, or the privacy
of their communications infringed.

58 Freedom of assembly and association
Every person has the freedom of assembly and association, and the freedom not to assemble or
associate.

59 Freedom to demonstrate and petition
Every person has the right to demonstrate and to present petitions, but must do so peacefully.

60 Freedom of conscience
(1) Every person has the right to freedom of conscience, which includes freedom of thought,
opinion, religion or belief.
(2) No person may be compelled to take an oath that is contrary to their religion or belief.

61 Freedom of expression and freedom of the media
(1) Every person has the right to freedom of expression, which includes freedom to seek, receive
and communicate ideas and other information, and freedom of artistic expression.
(2) Freedom of the media includes freedom from interference and protection of sources.
(3) Broadcasting and other media must be free to determine independently the editorial content
of their broadcasts or other communications.

62 Access to information
Every citizen and permanent resident has the right of access to any information held by the State
in so far as the information is required in the interests of public accountability.

65 Labour rights
(1) Every person has the right to fair and safe labour practices and standards and to be paid a
fair and reasonable wage.
(2) Except for members of the security services, every employee has the right to form and join a
trade union and to strike.

66 Freedom of movement and residence
Every Zimbabwean citizen and everyone else who is legally in Zimbabwe has the right to move
freely within Zimbabwe, to reside in any part of the country and to leave it.

67 Political rights
(1) Every Zimbabwean citizen has the right to free, fair and regular elections for any elective
public office.
(2) Every citizen has the right to form, to join and to participate in the activities of a
political party or organisation of their choice.

69 Right to a fair hearing
(1) Every person has the right to a fair, speedy and public hearing within a reasonable time
before an independent and impartial court.
(3) Every person accused of an offence has the right to a fair and public trial within a
reasonable time before an independent and impartial court.
(4) Every person has a right, at their own expense, to choose and be represented by a legal
practitioner before any court, tribunal or forum.

70 Rights of accused persons
Any person accused of an offence must be presumed innocent until proved guilty, must be informed
promptly of the charge and must have adequate time and facilities to prepare a defence.

71 Property rights
(1) Every person has the right, in any part of Zimbabwe, to acquire, hold, occupy, use, transfer
and dispose of all forms of property.
(3) No person may be compulsorily deprived of property except under a law of general
application, and only for a public purpose, with fair and adequate compensation paid promptly.

73 Right to environment
Every person has the right to an environment that is not harmful to their health or well-being,
and to have the environment protected for present and future generations.

75 Right to education
(1) Every citizen and permanent resident of Zimbabwe has a right to a basic State-funded
education, including adult basic education, and to further education which the State must make
progressively available.

76 Right to health care
(1) Every citizen and permanent resident of Zimbabwe has the right to have access to basic
health-care services, including reproductive health-care services.
(3) No person may be refused emergency medical treatment at any health institution.

77 Right to food and water
Every person has the right to safe, clean and potable water and to sufficient food, and the State
must take reasonable legislative and other measures to achieve these rights.

78 Marriage rights
(1) Every person who has attained the age of eighteen years has the right to found a family.
(2) No person may be compelled to enter into marriage against their will.

81 Rights of children
(1) Every child, that is to say every boy and girl under the age of eighteen years, has the right
to equal treatment before the law, to the prompt provision of a birth certificate, and to
education, health care services, nutrition and shelter.
(2) A child's best interests are paramount in every matter concerning the child.

85 Enforcement of fundamental human rights and freedoms
(1) Any of the following persons may approach a court, alleging that a fundamental right has been
infringed: any person acting in their own interests; any person acting on behalf of another who
cannot act for themselves; any person acting as a member, or in the interests, of a group or class
of persons; any person acting in the public interest; any association acting in the interests of
its members.

CHAPTER 5
EXECUTIVE AUTHORITY

91 Qualifications for election as President
(1) A person qualifies for election as President if they are a Zimbabwean citizen by birth or
descent, have attained the age of forty years, are ordinarily resident in Zimbabwe and are
registered as a voter.
(2) A person may not serve as President for more than two terms.

95 Term of office of President
(1) The President's term of office is five years and begins when the President is sworn in.
(2) The limit of two terms applies whether or not the terms are continuous.

96 Resignation of President
The President may resign by written notice to the Speaker, who must give public notice of the
resignation as soon as possible and in any event within twenty-four hours.

CHAPTER 7
ELECTIONS

143 Term of Parliament
Parliament is elected for a five-year term that runs from the date on which the President-elect
is sworn in and assumes office.

CHAPTER 8
JUDICIARY

164 Judicial independence
(1) The courts are independent and are subject only to this Constitution and the law, which they
must apply impartially, expeditiously and without fear, favour or prejudice.
(3) An order or decision of a court binds the State and all persons and governmental institutions.
